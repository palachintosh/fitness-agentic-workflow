# Progression & Tracking Plan

- **Owner:** progression-planner
- **Status:** complete
- **Updated:** 2026-10-07
- **Duration:** 12+ weeks of progressions

## Progression Philosophy

Your flexibility gains will come from consistent, gentle daily work rather than aggressive pushing. Beginners typically see noticeable improvement within 2–3 weeks and significant progress by week 6–8. This plan provides a pathway for 12+ weeks of sustainable progress.

---

## 12-Week Progression Pathway

### **Weeks 1–2: Foundation & Baseline (Current Week Pattern)**

**Goal:** Establish baseline range of motion and learn proper form

**What to do:**
- Follow the exact program provided (Days 1–7, repeating)
- Focus on form and breathing, not depth of stretch
- Record your starting hand position relative to your back

**Baseline Assessment (Record Week 1):**
- Can your fingers touch your lower back when reaching behind?
- How far apart are your hands?
- What's the furthest you can comfortably reach?
- Take a photo or note hand position for reference

**Key indicator:** You should feel mild stretch sensation, never pain

**Progression marker:** Hands may reach slightly further; mild improvements in range expected by end of week 2

---

### **Weeks 3–4: Initial Gains & Deepening**

**Goal:** Increase range of motion gradually; feel noticeably better

**Modifications to the program:**
- Increase static hold times by 5–10 seconds (e.g., 35–40 seconds instead of 30)
- Add 1 extra repetition to band work exercises
- In wall-assisted back reach: move hands slightly lower or closer together

**Expected progress:**
- Hands reach further back; gap between hands narrows
- Stretches feel easier; less initial resistance
- First noticeable improvement in your target (hands closer to back)

**Tracking:** Record hand position again at end of week 4

**Motivation note:** By week 4, most people report improved range; you're likely to see tangible progress

---

### **Weeks 5–6: Challenge Phase & Acceleration**

**Goal:** Push toward your primary goal (hand touches lower back)

**Modifications:**
- Increase hold times to 45–60 seconds on main stretches
- Band-assisted back reach: narrow hand spacing by 2–3 inches if possible
- Wall-assisted back reach: hands move 3–4 inches lower or wider
- Add 2–3 extra repetitions to band pull-aparts and wall slides

**Expected progress:**
- Hands likely touch or nearly touch back for many people
- Significant improvement in wall-assisted back reach
- Fewer tight spots; greater ease of movement

**Success milestone:** If your hands reach your lower back by now, excellent! You've met the primary goal.

**If not yet:** Continue; progress is still normal. Some people need weeks 7–8.

---

### **Weeks 7–8: Refinement & Consistency**

**Goal:** Solidify gains; build from where weeks 5–6 landed

**Modifications:**
- If you've reached your goal (back touch): maintain current depth and hold times
- If still working toward goal: continue gradual hand spacing narrowing (band work) and wall reach lowering
- Add dynamic variations: circles, slow rotations with bands
- Hold stretches: 60+ seconds where comfortable

**Expected progress:**
- By week 8, nearly all beginners should have hands touching lower back
- Continued ease of movement and reduced muscle tightness
- Greater confidence in reaching and holding positions

**Tracking:** Full assessment—hand position photo, range-of-motion notes

---

### **Weeks 9–10: Consolidation**

**Goal:** Maintain gains; add subtle intensity without major changes

**Modifications:**
- Maintain current hold times (60 seconds)
- Add one new exercise variation (e.g., single-arm band work, wider wall slides)
- Introduce very light band resistance upgrades if current band feels easy
- Explore end-range holds: push slightly into mild discomfort (not pain), hold 10 seconds, release

**Expected progress:**
- Continued ease and confidence in all movements
- Potential for hands to clasp or interlock behind back (advanced milestone)
- Overall shoulder and arm mobility continues improving

---

### **Weeks 11–12: Advancement & Preparation for Maintenance**

**Goal:** Build toward maintenance phase; continue or introduce new challenges

**Modifications:**
- If bands feel easy: upgrade to next resistance level
- Extended holds: 90 seconds on select main stretches
- Introduce antagonist work: light resistance band external rotation exercises (strengthening rear delts)
- Practice end-range holds with band assistance: 15–20 second holds at furthest comfortable point

**Expected progress:**
- Greater strength and stability in reached positions
- Hands may move closer together or interlock behind back
- Confidence in daily practice; flexibility becomes effortless

---

## Maintenance Phase (Week 13+)

**After 12 weeks**, you've built a solid foundation. Continue the 1-week program repeating, but:

- **Maintain, don't regress:** Continue 5–6 days per week to hold gains
- **Weekly variation:** Rotate band resistance or try new exercise variations every 2–3 weeks for continued improvement
- **Listen to your body:** If tight after days off, return to week 1–2 intensity before advancing

## Tracking Your Progress

### Primary Metric: Back-Reach Position
**Measurement method:**
1. Reach both hands behind your back, palms facing each other
2. Note how far apart your hands are (or if they touch/clasp)
3. Record weekly or bi-weekly (same time each week)
4. Take photos from behind: shows hand position clearly

### Secondary Metrics

**Stretch Hold Duration:**
- Week 1: Can comfortably hold 30-second stretches
- Week 6: Can hold 45–60 second stretches
- Week 12: Can hold 60–90 second stretches

**Ease of Movement:**
- Rate on 1–10 scale (1 = very tight/restricted, 10 = completely free)

- Week 6: Likely 6–7
- Week 12: Likely 8–9

**Consistency:**
- Track days per week you complete the program
- Goal: 6–7 days weekly
- Record any missed days and reason

## Regression & Modification Guidance

### When to Reduce Intensity

**Reduce back to previous week's intensity if:**
- You experience sharp pain during or after sessions
- Soreness persists for 2+ days after training
- You feel swelling in shoulders or arms
- You notice reduced range of motion (going backward)

**How to modify:**
1. Return to holds that felt comfortable (e.g., 30-second stretches instead of 60)
2. Remove extra repetitions added in recent weeks
3. Use lighter band resistance
4. Take an extra rest day during the week

### Soreness vs. Pain

**Normal flexibility training soreness:**
- Mild muscle tightness or slight discomfort during stretches
- Subsides within a few hours to 1 day
- Doesn't worsen the next day

**This is OK. Continue training.** Light gentle work often helps soreness resolve faster.

**Pain requiring modification:**
- Sharp, acute sensation during or immediately after stretching
- Swelling or visible inflammation
- Pain that worsens the day after training
- Persistent ache that doesn't fade

**Stop that exercise.** Modify or rest. If pain persists beyond 2–3 days, consult a healthcare provider.

### Progression Modifications (If Injury or Tightness Occurs)

1. **Reduce hold times:** 20 seconds instead of 30–45
2. **Reduce repetitions:** 8–10 instead of 12–15
3. **Use lighter band:** Switch to a lighter resistance level
4. **Increase rest days:** Take 1–2 extra rest days weekly
5. **Focus on gentle:** Emphasize Days 4 and 7 (release and recovery work)

Return to normal progression once 3–5 days of easy sessions feel pain-free.

---

## Deload Strategy (Every 4 Weeks)

**Weeks 4, 8, 12:** Intentionally reduce intensity to allow adaptation and prevent overuse

**During a deload week:**
- Reduce hold times by 50% (e.g., 15–20 seconds instead of 45)
- Reduce repetitions by 50%
- Reduce band work (or use lighter band)
- Emphasize release work (tennis ball, gentle stretching)
- Add extra rest day (5 days of training instead of 7)

**Purpose:** Allows your nervous system and connective tissue to adapt; prevents overuse injury; builds toward next progression phase

**Timing example:**
- Weeks 1–3: Normal intensity
- Week 4: Deload (lighter)
- Weeks 5–7: Return to normal
- Week 8: Deload (lighter)
- And so on...

## Clear Stopping Rules & Warning Signs

### Stop Immediately If:
- **Sharp, acute pain** in shoulder, arm, or neck (not mild stretch sensation, but sharp pain)
- **Sudden swelling** in shoulder, arm, or hand
- **Numbness or tingling** in fingers or hand (suggests nerve compression)
- **Radiating pain** down arm (suggests nerve involvement)
- **Clicking or popping** sounds accompanied by pain

### Action if warning signs occur:
1. **Stop the current exercise immediately**
2. **Rest for 2–3 days** without the problematic movement
3. **Ice if swollen** (10 minutes, 2–3 times daily)
4. **Gentle movement only** during rest days
5. **Consult a healthcare provider if symptoms persist** beyond 3 days

### Do Not Return to Full Training Until:
- Pain completely resolves
- No swelling present
- Full range of motion returns
- You can perform gentle movements pain-free for 2+ days

## Weekly Check-In Routine

Every Sunday, spend 2 minutes on:
1. Record hand-position photo or note
2. Rate ease of movement (1–10)
3. Note any missed days and reason
4. Reflect: Did stretches feel easier this week? Any improvements?
5. Plan: Any modifications for next week?

This simple tracking keeps you connected to progress and helps identify any issues early.
