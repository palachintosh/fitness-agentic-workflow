## 12-Week Progression Framework

| Week | Phase | Focus | Volume adjustment | Intensity adjustment | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|-------------------|----------------------|-------|
| 1–3 | Foundation | Establish baseline form and neural adaptation | 100% | RPE 6–7/10 (2–3 reps in reserve) | Conservative loads; technique priority |
| 4 | Deload | Recovery and consolidation | 50% | RPE 4–5/10 (4–5 reps in reserve) | Active recovery week; lighter loads |
| 5–8 | Hypertrophy-Strength | Increase load and volume | 110–120% | RPE 7–8/10 (1–2 reps in reserve) | Progressive overload; measurable strength gains |
| 9 | Deload | Recovery and adaptation | 50% | RPE 4–5/10 (4–5 reps in reserve) | Mid-program recovery; prevent burnout |

## Regression and Recovery Rules

### Missed Session Protocol

If you miss 1 training session:
- No load/rep adjustment needed; resume at next scheduled session

If you miss 2–3 consecutive sessions:
- Reduce all loads by 10% on return
- Return to the appropriate week of your progression cycle
- Use RPE 6/10 (3 reps in reserve) for first session back

If you miss 4+ consecutive sessions (>1 week):
- Return using Week 1–3 baseline loads (conservative phase)
- Spend 1–2 weeks rebuilding before returning to your progression phase

### Progression Stall Protocol

If you cannot increase weight or reps for 2 consecutive weeks on the same exercise:
- Hold current load and reps for 1 additional week
- In week 4, reduce load by 5% and increase reps by 2 (e.g., 12 lbs × 8 reps → 10 lbs × 10 reps)
- Use this lighter load to practice form and accumulate volume
- Return to original load when reps reach 12–15

If you plateau for 3+ weeks despite consistent effort:
- Reduce load by 10–15%
- Use higher rep range (12–15 reps) for 2–3 weeks
- Gradually increase load while maintaining reps
- Consider switching to resistance band variation for that exercise (lighter stimulus)

### Joint Pain or Discomfort Protocol

If you experience joint pain during or after an exercise:
- Stop immediately; do not push through pain
- Skip that exercise for the current session
- Use the substitution exercise from equipment-plan.md
- Wait 3–5 days before attempting the painful exercise again
- If pain returns, switch to the substitution exercise for 2+ weeks
- Consider reducing load by 20–30% and rebuilding gradually
- If pain persists beyond 2 weeks, discontinue that exercise

### Return-from-Illness Protocol

If you take a break due to illness (1–2 weeks):
- Use deload-week parameters: 50% load, 2 sets per exercise, RPE 4–5/10
- Spend 2–3 sessions rebuilding at this reduced intensity
- Return to your progression phase gradually over the next 1–2 weeks

If you take a break due to illness (>2 weeks):
- Return using Week 1–3 baseline loads (conservative phase)
- Spend 2–3 weeks rebuilding before resuming progression
- Reduce load by 20–30% if returning after 3+ weeks

## Deload Week Protocol

### Week 4 & Week 9 Deload Guidelines

- **Volume reduction:** Cut working sets in half (3 sets → 2 sets)
- **Load reduction:** Use 60–70% of current working weight
- **Rep range:** Maintain standard rep range (6–12 reps)
- **RPE:** Drop to 4–5/10 (easy session, 4–5 reps in reserve)
- **Purpose:** Active recovery, technique refinement, injury prevention
- **Session structure:** Warm-up (5 min) → 2 sets per exercise → Cooldown (5 min)

### Deload Week Rationale

Deload weeks prevent accumulated fatigue, reduce injury risk, and prepare the body for the next progression phase. This is especially important for beginners adding load progressively.

## Success Criteria and Exit Goals

### Baseline Measurements (End of Week 1)

- Dumbbell Bicep Curl: 8 lbs × 8–10 reps
- Overhead Tricep Extension: 8 lbs × 8–10 reps
- Dumbbell Shoulder Press: 8 lbs × 8–10 reps
- Lateral Raise: 3 lbs × 10–12 reps
- Dumbbell Hammer Curl: 8 lbs × 8–10 reps

### Success Criteria (End of Week 12)

Program is successful if you achieve **at least one** of the following:

| Exercise | Success goal | Measurement |
|----------|--------------|-------------|
| Dumbbell Bicep Curl | Increase from 8 lbs → 15–20 lbs | Double the load; same or higher reps |
| Overhead Tricep Extension | Increase from 8 lbs → 15 lbs | Nearly double the load |
| Dumbbell Shoulder Press | Increase from 8 lbs → 15 lbs | Nearly double the load |
| Lateral Raise | Increase from 3 lbs → 8–10 lbs | Triple the load |
| Dumbbell Hammer Curl | Increase from 8 lbs → 15–20 lbs | Double the load |
| Close-Grip Push-ups | Increase reps from 8 → 15+ | 50%+ increase in reps |

### Strength Gain Interpretation

- **150%+ of baseline load:** Exceptional progress; meet or exceed all criteria
- **120–150% of baseline load:** Strong progress; meet 4+ criteria
- **100–120% of baseline load:** Solid progress; meet 2–3 criteria
- **Below 100%:** Reassess program adherence, sleep, nutrition, recovery

### Beyond Week 12

After week 12, continue with the Week 10–12 progression template:
- Add load 2–5 lbs weekly when 6 reps becomes achievable
- Maintain 1 deload week every 4 weeks
- Rotate exercise variations to prevent boredom and plateaus
- Consider moving to upper-body splits if arm gains plateau

## Progression Troubleshooting

### If you cannot add load for 2+ weeks

1. Verify form and full range of motion
2. Check recovery (sleep, nutrition, stress)
3. Reduce load 10% and rebuild; do not push through pain
4. Consider extending current phase by 1–2 weeks

### If you experience joint pain

1. Stop the exercise immediately
2. Reduce load to 50% and use higher reps (12–15 range)
3. If pain persists, skip exercise and use substitution from equipment-plan.md
4. Resume gradually after 1–2 weeks of lower load

### If you plateau

1. Verify you are at RPE 7–8/10 (not under-training)
2. Try adding 1–2 reps before adding load
3. Use resistance band version of exercise as variation
4. Consider single-arm variations for additional stimulus
