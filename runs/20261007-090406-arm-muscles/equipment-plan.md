## Recommended Equipment Shopping List

### Primary Equipment

#### 1. Adjustable Dumbbells (5-35 lbs per hand)
- **Specifications:** 
  - Weight range: 5 lbs to 35 lbs per dumbbell (adjustable in 5 lb increments)
  - Type: Selector or pin-and-weight adjustable system
  - Set includes: Two dumbbells (minimum)
- **Primary movements:** Bicep curls, tricep extensions, shoulder presses, lateral raises
- **Beginner rationale:** Adjustable dumbbells allow progressive overload (fundamental for strength gains); low footprint for home storage; safe for beginners to learn proper form
- **Estimated cost:** $100-$250 USD for quality pair (5-35 lb range)
- **Where to source:** Dick's Sporting Goods, Amazon, Walmart, Target, local sporting goods retailers
- **Setup tips for beginners:**
  - Start at 5-10 lbs per dumbbell to practice form
  - Keep dumbbells in a dedicated corner or small rack
  - Allow 2-3 feet of space per side for safe arm movement
  - Avoid using on unstable surfaces (balance on dumbbells is not safe)

#### 2. Resistance Bands (Light to Medium resistance)
- **Specifications:**
  - Type: Loop bands or tube bands with handles
  - Resistance levels: Light (1-5 lbs equivalent) and Medium (5-15 lbs equivalent)
  - Set includes: Minimum 2 bands of different resistances
- **Primary movements:** Bicep curls, tricep extensions, shoulder raises, rows (when anchored)
- **Beginner rationale:** Bands provide accommodating resistance (easier at start of movement, harder at end); excellent for form learning; portable; very affordable
- **Estimated cost:** $15-$40 USD for quality set of 2-3 bands
- **Where to source:** Amazon, Walmart, Target, Dick's Sporting Goods, online fitness retailers
- **Setup tips for beginners:**
  - Store in a small bag to keep organized
  - Check bands for tears or damage before each session
  - Loop bands can be anchored under feet or tied to door anchors
  - Start with light resistance to learn movement patterns
  - Bands are quieter than dumbbells (good for shared spaces)

### Optional (Secondary) Equipment

#### 3. Resistance Band Door Anchor (if using loop bands)
- **Specifications:** Over-the-door hook or anchor system
- **Cost:** $8-$15 USD
- **Purpose:** Enables rows, lat pulldowns, high-to-low movements without additional equipment
- **Setup tip:** Install on any hinged door; test stability before use with resistance

#### 4. Pull-up Bar (optional for advanced progression)
- **Specifications:** Doorway pull-up bar or wall-mounted option
- **Cost:** $25-$100 USD
- **Note:** Not necessary for beginner arm training; flagged for future progression only

## Substitution Rules

### SUB-01: Bicep Curl

- **Preferred equipment option:** Adjustable dumbbells (8-25 lbs)
- **Reduced-equipment option:** Resistance bands (medium resistance loop)
- **Bodyweight option:** None (bicep curl specifically requires external load)
- **Avoid when:** Using dumbbells that are too heavy for controlled form (beginner should use weight allowing 8-12 reps with good technique)
- **Safety-review flag:** no

**Notes:** Dumbbells provide most stable and progressive load path. Bands are excellent for deload weeks or form work. If neither available, skip and focus on other arm movements.

### SUB-02: Tricep Extension (Overhead)

- **Preferred equipment option:** Single adjustable dumbbell (8-20 lbs)
- **Reduced-equipment option:** Resistance band (medium loop)
- **Bodyweight option:** Tricep dips using sturdy chair (see SUB-03 for safety notes)
- **Avoid when:** Using dumbbell that causes elbow strain or uncontrolled lowering
- **Safety-review flag:** no

**Notes:** Single heavy dumbbell works better than two light ones for this movement. Bands provide good alternative for learning movement pattern.

### SUB-03: Tricep Dip

- **Preferred equipment option:** Parallel bars or sturdy, stable bench
- **Reduced-equipment option:** Resistance bands anchored low (assists dips to reduce bodyweight load)
- **Bodyweight option:** Negative reps only (jump to top position, lower slowly) if furniture cannot support full dip
- **Avoid when:** Using unstable chair, wobbly furniture, or surfaces that could collapse
- **Safety-review flag:** yes

**Notes:** Dips are effective but carry injury risk if surface is unstable. Beginners should test furniture thoroughly. If uncertain, prioritize dumbbells and bands instead. Negative dips (lowering portion only) are safer alternative for building strength.

### SUB-04: Shoulder Press

- **Preferred equipment option:** Pair of adjustable dumbbells (10-25 lbs)
- **Reduced-equipment option:** Resistance bands (medium to heavy loop)
- **Bodyweight option:** None (requires external load for effective strength training)
- **Avoid when:** Pressing weight that causes pain in shoulder joint or compromised form
- **Safety-review flag:** no

**Notes:** Dumbbells allow independent arm stabilization; safer than barbell for beginners. Bands provide good alternative for lighter loads or deload work.

### SUB-05: Lateral Raise

- **Preferred equipment option:** Adjustable dumbbells (3-12 lbs)
- **Reduced-equipment option:** Resistance bands (light to medium loop)
- **Bodyweight option:** None (requires external load)
- **Avoid when:** Using dumbbells that require shoulder shrugging or momentum to lift
- **Safety-review flag:** no

**Notes:** Lateral raises use lighter loads; start at 3-5 lbs per dumbbell. Excellent form is critical. Bands provide low-cost alternative for learning proper movement pattern.

### SUB-06: Barbell Curl (if exercise-research recommends)

- **Preferred equipment option:** NOT AVAILABLE for home setup (requires barbell, plates, rack)
- **Reduced-equipment option:** Dumbbell curl (single or pair)
- **Bodyweight option:** None
- **Avoid when:** Attempting to improvise a barbell (unsafe)
- **Safety-review flag:** no

**Notes:** Barbell curl has high barrier to entry for home training. Dumbbell curls are safer for beginners and provide equivalent stimulus. Substitute without hesitation.
