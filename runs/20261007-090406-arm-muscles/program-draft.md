## Program Summary

- Primary goal: Build greater arm strength (measurable through lifting capacity)
- Program duration: 12 weeks
- Sessions per week: 5 days per week
- Maximum session duration: 45 minutes per session
- Experience level: Beginner
- Training location: Home
- Available equipment: Adjustable dumbbells (5–35 lbs), resistance bands
- Design approach: Split-based arm-focused program rotating through biceps, triceps, shoulders, and forearms. Conservative starting loads appropriate to beginner status. Two complete rest days (weekends) for recovery. Each session includes targeted warm-up, 4–5 exercises with 3 sets each, and cooldown.

## Workload and Recovery Check

**Major Movement Coverage:**
- **Biceps:** S-01, S-04, S-05 (4 dedicated sessions; balanced frequency for strength gains)
- **Triceps:** S-02, S-03, S-05 (3 dedicated sessions; ample stimulus for antagonist development)
- **Shoulders (deltoids):** S-02, S-04, S-05 (3 dedicated sessions; pressing and lateral movement variation)
- **Forearms:** S-01, S-04, S-05 (reverse curls; secondary activation during bicep work)
- **Back (lats, upper back):** S-03 (1 session; rows support arm pulling strength and posture)

**Repeated High-Demand Movements:**
- Dumbbell Shoulder Press: S-02 (8–10 reps), S-05 (8–10 reps) — separated by 3 days; moderate repeat frequency appropriate for strength development
- Dumbbell Bicep Curl: S-01 (8–10 reps), S-04 (10–12 reps) — separated by 3 days; primary exercise repeated with intensity variation (lower reps M, higher reps Th)
- Tricep work (varied): S-02 (overhead extension), S-03 (push-up + extension + kickback), S-05 (kickback) — distributed across 3 separate days; no same exercise on adjacent days
- Lateral Raise: S-01 (10–12 reps), S-02 (10–12 reps) — separated by 1 day (adjacent); low absolute load (3–5 lbs) mitigates recovery concern

**Recovery Spacing:**
- Biceps: Monday (S-01), Thursday (S-04) = 3-day recovery (sufficient for strength adaptation)
- Shoulders: Tuesday (S-02), Thursday (S-04), Friday (S-05) = varied spacing (2–1 days); loads adjusted to ensure adequate recovery (RPE 6–7/10)
- Triceps: Tuesday (S-02), Wednesday (S-03), Friday (S-05) = distributed; Wednesday session is tricep-focused but includes opposing back work (row) to balance joints
- Two full rest days (Saturday, Sunday) enable systemic recovery before Monday restart

**Starting-Volume Rationale:**
- Total exercises per session: 4–5 exercises
- Sets per exercise: 3 sets (industry standard for strength development in beginner phase)
- Repetitions: 8–15 reps (appropriate range for strength and hypertrophy for beginner; higher reps later in week allow CNS recovery)
- Load: Dumbbells 8–10 lbs (primary arm work), 5–8 lbs (secondary), 3–5 lbs (isolation/shoulders) — conservative for beginner per exercise-research guidance
- Session volume: ~40 minutes work + rest (within 45-min limit) — sustainable frequency for 5 days/week
- Weekly density: Approximately 60 sets per week across all arm muscles — moderate volume for beginner adaptation without overtraining

## Notes on Design Decisions

1. **Five-Day Split Rationale:** Distributes arm muscle groups across the week (biceps Monday/Thursday, triceps Tuesday/Wednesday/Friday, shoulders Tuesday/Thursday/Friday, forearms throughout) to balance volume and recovery while meeting the 5-day/week requirement.

2. **Conservative Loading:** All exercises begin at the lower end of beginner progression ranges (e.g., 8 lbs for press/curl, 3–5 lbs for lateral raise) per exercise-research guidance. This ensures sustainable form development and adaptation across 12 weeks.

3. **RPE 6–7/10 Intensity:** Targets 2–4 reps in reserve. This conservative approach is appropriate for beginners and allows for sustainable 5-day-per-week training without CNS overtraining.

4. **Inclusion of Back Work (Bent-Over Row):** Although goal is arm strength, rows support pulling-pattern strength and arm development (secondary bicep activation). Also improves posture and balances pressing work, reducing shoulder injury risk.

5. **Close-Grip Push-ups as Tricep Stimulus:** Bodyweight exercise reduces equipment dependency and provides functional pressing strength alongside dumbbell work.

6. **Substitution Coverage:** Every exercise includes 1–2 alternatives (band versions, modified reps, or volume reductions) to ensure adaptability if equipment becomes unavailable or discomfort arises mid-program.

7. **Cooldown Specificity:** Each session cooldown targets the primary muscle groups worked, using static stretching to support recovery rather than generic routines.

8. **No Progression Prescription:** Program defines only Week 1 baseline. Progression-planner will specify load increases, rep targets, and adaptation strategies for weeks 2–12.
